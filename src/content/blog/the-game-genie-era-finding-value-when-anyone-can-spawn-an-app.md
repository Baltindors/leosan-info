So, here is the existential crisis of the week: If everyone is a "builder" now, what exactly is a Product Manager supposed to do? If the barrier to shipping code has dropped to zero, are we just highly paid cheerleaders?

Short answer: No. But if your entire job was just acting as a human router—taking business requests, formatting them into Jira tickets, and nagging frontend devs about padding—you are officially an NPC in this new meta.

Here is how you level up and find meaning when execution is completely commoditized.

### 1. Writing is Thinking (Stop Outsourcing Your Brain)

There is a massive temptation to just ask an LLM to "write a PRD for a new user onboarding flow." Product legend Marty Cagan recently pointed out the fatal flaw here: writing a PRD _is_ the definition of thinking. If you outsource the narrative, you outsource your product sense. AI is here to augment your intelligence, not replace it. Use AI to challenge your assumptions, find edge cases in your logic, or summarize user research, but do not let it define the core problem. The moment you stop thinking critically about _why_ you are building something, your value drops to zero.

### 2. Code is a Commodity; Product Sense is Premium

Kris Gale famously said, "The value is in what gets used, not in what gets built". In the past, 90% of a company's effort was spent just wrestling with the compiler to get the software to exist. Now that generating the software is cheap, the penalty for building the _wrong_ thing isn't necessarily lost dev hours—it's lost user trust and flooded markets.

Your job is no longer to manage the pipeline of output; your job is to relentlessly filter the noise and apply world-class Product Sense. You have to "fall in love with the problem, not the solution". Anyone can spawn a shiny new app, but very few know if it actually solves a bleeding-neck problem for the user.

### 3. You Are Now the Editor-in-Chief (and the QA Boss)

When everyone can generate an app, the differentiator becomes quality and reliability. LLMs hallucinate. APIs break. Data models drift. As an AI PM, your new superpower is Evaluation. You need to understand the unit economics of a feature (how much does this API call cost in tokens?), where the model is likely to fail, and how to design graceful fallbacks for the user.

You aren't necessarily writing the Python scripts, but you own the trade-offs. You are the one building the evaluation sets—the 20 weird edge cases that prove the AI isn't going to accidentally leak a customer's data or give them insanely wrong advice.

### 4. Empathy Doesn't Have an API

An LLM can write a flawless sorting algorithm for an Express backend, but it cannot sit in a room with a frustrated user, watch their shoulders slump when a dashboard fails to load, and understand their emotional state. That human context is the ultimate moat.

We are in the golden era of product management. We finally get to stop managing the _factory_ and start managing the _product_. Grab a controller, player one.

### TL;DR

When AI makes building apps practically free, code becomes a commodity and Product Sense becomes the premium. Stop acting as a human Jira router and start acting as an Editor-in-Chief. Don't use AI to do your thinking (like writing PRDs); use it to test your logic. The real value is in knowing _what_ to build, setting up strict evaluations for AI features, and intimately understanding the user's pain—because empathy doesn't have an API.
